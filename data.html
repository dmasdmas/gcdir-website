What’s in it

Two halves that are rarely in the same room.

For each campaign, we know what the audience did afterwards: purchases, sign-ups and revenue. For each creator, we know who they are, psychologically. Put the two together and you can ask which creators persuade, and then check.

Records like these usually stay inside brands and platforms. As far as we know, few academic studies have worked with them at this scale.

The data are harmonized across brands, so a result from skincare can be set beside one from sportswear. A single brand’s data can’t do that.

Campaigns
About 230,000 commercial influencer campaigns with outcome data, from more than 800 brands in consumer categories
Creators
Psychometric and behavioral profiles of about 10 million content creators, across the major platforms and markets
Measures
About 160 standardized signals per profiled creator, including personality, values, way of talking and audience characteristics
Period
More than ten years of campaigns, harmonized to one measurement framework
Outcomes
Sales, sign-ups, revenue and related transaction-level records

Where it comes from

Trudy, disclosed.

Trudy is software that predicts which creators will sell for a brand. The campaign records and creator profiles it is built on are the data we study.

Several GCDIR members also work at Trudy. Every publication states where its data came from, who paid for what, and each author’s affiliations. Trudy can check a manuscript for confidential business or personal data before submission. It cannot change a finding or stop a paper.
