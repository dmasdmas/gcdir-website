Primary research program

The psychology of persuasion in creator-mediated environments

The Center’s principal program examines the conditions under which content produced by individuals on social platforms shapes the attitudes, trust and behavior of their audiences.

It draws on GCDIR’s access to large-scale behavioral and outcome data that links creator-level psychometric profiles to measured downstream behavior.

What makes it distinctive

Access to validated behavioral outcomes, including conversion and revenue data from commercial campaigns, rather than the engagement proxies that typically constrain research in this area.

Questions the program addresses

  1. How creator psychological characteristics — values, personality, communication style — relate to audience response
  2. How trust and parasocial influence form in feed-based environments
  3. Congruency effects: how similarity between creators and their audiences shapes engagement, persuasion and behavioral outcomes
  4. When exposure to creator content produces measurable behavioral change, including but not limited to commercial outcomes
  5. Methods for the empirical study of psychological influence at scale

Broader research interests

Adjacent questions in digital influence

The Center engages with a range of related questions, both through its own work and through collaboration with affiliated researchers, particularly where GCDIR’s data resources or methods can support broader scholarly inquiry.
