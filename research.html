Main program

The psychology of persuasion in creator-mediated environments

That is the formal name. In plain words, it asks which creators change what their audiences think and do, and why some are far more persuasive than others.

It runs on our main data resource, which links psychological profiles of creators to the sales, sign-ups and revenue from their campaigns. The Data page says what the resource holds and how access works.

We combine behavioral science with statistics and computational methods. A hunch is where a study starts, never where it ends.

Five questions we keep asking

  1. Which traits of a creator, such as their values, personality and way of talking, predict how their audience responds?
  2. How do people come to trust someone they only know from a feed, and what does that trust do?
  3. Does it help when a creator resembles their audience? When does it stop helping?
  4. When does watching a creator lead to a change in behavior that you can actually measure?
  5. How do you measure influence reliably across millions of creators and campaigns?

Ongoing2025–

Birds of a Feather or Digital Echo Chambers: Congruency in Influencer Marketing

Do audiences respond more to creators who resemble them? The project measures how alike a creator and their audience are, psychologically and demographically, and relates that to engagement and sales in commercial campaigns.

The hunch is that persuasion takes two. The project checks how much the pair matters, and where similarity stops helping.
