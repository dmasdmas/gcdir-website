Who do people believe online, and why?

Our approach

Likes are easy to count. So everyone counts them.

Views, likes and shares are public and plentiful. They say very little about whether anybody was persuaded.

We work with records of what audiences did after a campaign, each linked to a profile of the creator who ran it. That lets us ask which creators change behavior, and then check the answer against what actually happened.

Inside a creator profile

Five traits, ten values and a way of talking.

Psychology spent the best part of a century boiling people down to a short list. We use the two lists that held up best, and add how a creator talks.

Main program

The psychology of persuasion in creator-mediated environments

In plain words: which creators change what their audiences do, and why. We relate creator profiles to what happened after their campaigns.

The program {% include areas.html %}
Current project2025–

Birds of a feather, or digital echo chambers?

Do audiences respond more to creators who resemble them? We measure how alike a creator and their audience are, psychologically and demographically, and relate it to engagement and sales in commercial campaigns.

The hunch: persuasion takes two. The project checks how much the pair matters.
